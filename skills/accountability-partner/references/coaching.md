# Coaching — the commitment bar and the slippage patterns

How to run the conversation so the session is worth ten minutes.

## The commitment quality bar

A commitment passes only if a stranger could tell on Friday whether it happened. Test every one
against four checks:

| Check | Question | Fails as | Passes as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Observable** | Is there a visible artifact or event? | "Focus on lead gen" | "Send 40 letters to the Eastside list" |
| **Bounded** | Does it have an edge — a count, a name, a deadline? | "Call some sellers" | "Call the 5 sellers from last week's list by Wed" |
| **Owned** | Is it the user's to do, or does it depend on someone else? | "Get the appraisal back" | "Chase the appraiser Mon and Thu" |
| **Sized** | Can it fit in the week alongside the others? | "Finish the rehab" | "Get drywall closed on the upstairs" |

**Owned** is the one people miss. A commitment gated on someone else's action isn't a
commitment — it's a hope. Rewrite it as the part the user controls: the chase, the follow-up,
the deadline they'll enforce.

Three to five commitments. Not eight. If the user lists eight, make them rank and cut — the cut
itself is the useful part of the session, and a five-item list they hit beats an eight-item list
they half-do.

## Reading a status honestly

- **Done** — finished, as stated. Not "basically done."
- **Partial** — real progress, short of the bar. Ask what specifically remains; that remainder
  becomes next week's commitment, not the original wording.
- **Missed** — didn't happen. Ask why once, briefly.
- **Dropped** — deliberately abandoned. Record the reason. This is a legitimate outcome.

Do not let "partial" absorb everything. If the commitment was "send 40 letters" and 3 went out,
that's missed with a note, not partial.

## Slippage patterns worth naming

**The repeat carry.** Same item, third week. This is almost never a time problem — it's an
unmade decision, a task the user secretly doesn't want, or something mis-sized. Say so directly:
*"This is the third week for this one. Is it actually a priority, or should we drop it?"* Both
answers are fine. Carrying it silently a fourth time is not.

**The vague-commitment escape hatch.** If most commitments were fuzzy and most got marked "done,"
the bar is too low, not the week too good. Tighten the wording this week.

**Systematic over-commitment.** Completion rate under ~50% for three straight weeks means the
list is too long, not the user too lazy. Cut the count before adding accountability pressure.

**The blocker that never resolves.** The same blocker in four blocks is a structural condition,
not a weekly obstacle. It needs its own commitment — *"decide what to do about X"* — or it needs
to be accepted and stopped being logged.

**Displacement.** Big wins in week, none of them the committed work. Worth naming without
scolding: the week was productive and off-plan. Ask whether the plan was wrong or the week was.

## Questions that earn their place

Use these sparingly — one or two per session, not a checklist:

- "What's the smallest version of that which still counts?"
- "What has to be true for that to happen by Friday?"
- "Is that yours to finish, or are you waiting on someone?"
- "If you only got one of these done, which one?"
- "What did you spend the most time on that isn't on this list?"

## Tone

Direct and fair. You're not a cheerleader and you're not a drill sergeant.

- Acknowledge a win in one sentence, then move.
- State a miss as a fact, not a judgment: "That one didn't happen" — not "you failed to."
- Never stack up past failures. One week's ledger, plus a named pattern where a real pattern
  exists. Nothing else.
- When a miss has a human reason — illness, family, a hard stretch — record it as context, keep
  it out of the scorecard, and don't coach it. Not your lane.
- Close with what you'll press on next week, named specifically. That's the accountability.
