# Finish Library — Materials by Tier & Category

This is the taste layer. The script picks the tier and the budget; this library
says what actually goes in the house. Pull the cell for the target tier in each
category, and let the buyer profile break ties on style.

## The one principle that governs everything

A buyer reads finishes as a signal of the whole house. Finishes that **match the
top of the comp set** say "move-in ready, priced right." Finishes a notch **above**
the comps rarely come back as price — they come back as the seller's lost margin.
Finishes **below** the comps make a buyer wonder what else was cheaped out, and
the house sits. Match the comps. The script's comp guard is there to keep you honest.

## Tier identities

- **Rental / Investor-grade** — durability and easy turnover over looks. Every
  choice is "what survives a tenant and a maintenance call."
- **Entry / Starter** — clean, bright, move-in ready for a first-time buyer on a
  budget. Nothing fancy, nothing offensive, nothing broken.
- **Mid-market** — the bread-and-butter flip. Broad appeal, current-but-safe
  finishes, the look most buyers picture when they imagine "renovated."
- **Move-up / Premium** — buyers trading up who expect quality and one or two
  "wow" moments. Cohesion and a sense of intention matter here.
- **Luxury / High-end** — design-forward, premium throughout, where the finishes
  themselves are part of what's being sold. Hire a designer at this tier.

## Flooring

| Tier | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Rental | Mid-grade luxury vinyl plank (LVP), 12 mil+ wear layer. One product through the whole unit. Tile in wet areas only if budget allows. |
| Entry | Quality LVP throughout main living; keep it one continuous color. Carpet only in bedrooms if the market expects it. |
| Mid | Wide-plank LVP or engineered wood in living areas; porcelain tile in baths. Consistency across the floor plan reads as "renovated." |
| Move-up | Engineered hardwood or premium wide-plank in living areas; large-format porcelain in baths; consider a tile feature in the primary bath. |
| Luxury | Real hardwood (site- or pre-finished), natural stone or large-format porcelain, custom tile work in baths. Material transitions detailed intentionally. |

Universal: minimize the number of distinct flooring materials. Two, maybe three
for the whole house. Transitions between them are where cheap jobs show.

## Interior paint

| Tier | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Rental | Single neutral wall color throughout, semi-gloss in wet areas for scrubbing. White trim/ceiling. Durability line. |
| Entry | Warm or cool neutral (greige/soft white) walls, white trim, flat-or-eggshell walls. Crisp, bright, consistent. |
| Mid | Current neutral (the market will tell you warm vs cool this year), white or near-white trim, an accent only if the comps have one. |
| Move-up | Designer neutral with subtle depth, considered trim color, possibly one tasteful accent wall or a moody powder room. |
| Luxury | Curated palette with depth and contrast, specialty finishes (limewash, millwork paint), color-matched to the design story. |

Universal: when in doubt, neutral. Bold paint is a taste bet, and you're selling
to a stranger. Save personality for fixtures and the front door.

## Kitchen — cabinets, counters, backsplash, appliances

| Tier | Cabinets | Counters | Backsplash | Appliances |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rental | Clean existing or stock flat-panel; new hardware | Laminate or entry quartz remnant | Paint or simple peel-and-stick only if needed | Basic black/white or clean existing |
| Entry | Refresh or stock shaker (white/light) | Entry-level quartz or quality laminate | Simple subway tile | New white or black suite, budget brand |
| Mid | Stock or semi-custom shaker, soft-close | Mid-grade quartz | Subway or stacked tile, simple | New stainless suite, mainstream brand |
| Move-up | Semi-custom, two-tone or island accent | Quality quartz, possibly waterfall island | Full-height or designer tile | Stainless suite, recognizable brand, maybe gas range |
| Luxury | Custom cabinetry, paneled appliances | Premium quartz or natural stone | Statement tile or slab backsplash | Pro-grade / paneled (designer-spec) |

The kitchen is where the most ARV is won or lost — it gets the biggest budget
slice for a reason. Don't half-do it; a kitchen that's clearly "almost" reads
worse than a clean, complete budget kitchen.

## Bathrooms

| Tier | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Rental | Reglaze tub, new toilet/vanity/faucet, simple tile surround, fresh caulk. Clean and watertight beats pretty. |
| Entry | New vanity, mirror, light bar, toilet; tub/shower with simple tile surround; porcelain floor. |
| Mid | New vanity (stone top), framed mirror, tiled shower or tub surround to ceiling, niche, modern fixtures. |
| Move-up | Double vanity in primary, tiled walk-in shower with glass, feature floor or wall tile, upgraded fixtures. |
| Luxury | Custom vanity, freestanding tub, frameless glass, full stone/large-format, heated floor, designer fixtures. |

## Lighting & fixtures

| Tier | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Rental | Clean, efficient, durable. LED flush mounts, basic faucets in a finish that hides wear. |
| Entry | Updated flush mounts, a simple pendant or two over an island/sink, matching faucet finishes throughout. |
| Mid | Coordinated lighting "family," recessed + pendants where it counts, one finish story for plumbing and hardware. |
| Move-up | Statement fixtures at entry/dining/island, layered lighting, consistent premium finish (matte black, brushed brass, etc.). |
| Luxury | Designer/spec lighting as jewelry, dimming/layered scenes, fully coordinated metals across the house. |

Universal: pick ONE metal finish story (or a deliberate two — e.g., black +
brass) and carry it across lighting, plumbing, and hardware. Mixed-by-accident
finishes are the fastest tell of an amateur flip.

## Curb appeal

| Tier | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Rental | Tidy: mow, mulch, trim, clean entry, working exterior light, fresh door paint. |
| Entry | Fresh door color, new house numbers/light/mailbox, mulch and a few shrubs, pressure-wash. |
| Mid | The above plus defined beds, a small porch moment, consistent exterior paint/trim, clean driveway. |
| Move-up | Landscaped beds, upgraded front door/hardware, exterior lighting design, possibly fresh siding accent or paint. |
| Luxury | Designed landscape, premium door and hardware, architectural lighting, the exterior reads as intentional from the curb. |

Curb appeal is the cheapest ARV per dollar at every tier — it sets the buyer's
expectation before they walk in. Never skip the front door and the entry light.

## Splurge vs. save (any tier)

- **Splurge:** kitchen (counters + a complete look), primary bath, the front
  door and entry, lighting that photographs well.
- **Save:** bedrooms (paint + flooring is enough), secondary spaces, anything a
  buyer won't notice in the first ten minutes.
- **Never cheap out on:** anything that signals "unfinished" or "will break" —
  half-tiled showers, mismatched metals, a clearly-budget kitchen in a mid+ comp
  set. Incompleteness reads worse than honest simplicity.
