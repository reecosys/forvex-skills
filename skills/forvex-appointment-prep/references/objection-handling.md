# Objection Handling

Structured responses to the objections HV franchisees hit most often in seller appointments. This closes a gap the HV Appointment Process v2.0 itself flags as a coaching need ("develop structured responses for common objections").

**How the skill uses this:** when prepping a brief, include 2–3 objections most likely for *this* seller's posture (based on Readvise motivation + asking-vs-MAO gap), with the framed response. Don't dump all of them — pick what fits. Post-meeting, if the franchisee says "they pushed back on X," pull the matching response.

## Framing principles (Sandler + Voss)

Every response should:

1. **Acknowledge before answering** (Voss tactical empathy — label the emotion/position first). Never argue.
2. **Anchor on rehab and certainty, not value.** Sellers argue ARV; they don't argue rehab costs or the value of a guaranteed close.
3. **Use calibrated questions** to make the seller solve the problem ("How am I supposed to get there given the work it needs?").
4. **Never get defensive about the number.** The number is the math. If it doesn't work for them, that's information, not a fight.
5. **Be willing to walk.** The strongest position is genuine willingness to leave. Don't negotiate against yourself.

## The objections

### "Your offer is too low"

**What's really being said:** Could be a real gap, or a reflexive opening move. Find out which.

**Response:**
> "You're right to push on that — it probably feels low compared to what you've seen online. Can I show you how I got there? [Walk through ARV − rehab − costs.] The number's driven by what it'll take to get it retail-ready. **What number were you hoping for, and how'd you land on it?**"

**Principle:** Acknowledge (Voss label), show the math anchored on rehab, then a calibrated question that surfaces *their* anchor and where it came from. Often reveals a Zestimate or a neighbor's sale that doesn't apply.

---

### "Zillow says it's worth way more than that"

**Response:**
> "Zillow's a starting point, but it's an algorithm that's never walked through your house — it doesn't know about [the roof / the kitchen / whatever you saw]. My number's based on actual sales of comparable homes nearby, minus what it costs to get this one to that condition. **Want me to show you the three comps I used?**"

**Principle:** Don't trash Zillow (seller may trust it). Reframe to comps + rehab. Offer transparency.

---

### "I owe more than that on the mortgage"

**What's really being said:** Hard floor below which they can't sell — or they think they can't.

**Response:**
> "That's an important thing to know. Two questions: **what's the exact payoff**, and **how flexible is the lender**? Sometimes there are options — a short sale, or sometimes the payoff is lower than people think once you call. If the numbers genuinely can't work, I'd rather tell you that than waste your time. **What's the situation that's making selling the right move right now?**"

**Principle:** This is often a real wall. Don't pretend otherwise. Surface the exact payoff, explore lender flexibility, and re-confirm motivation. If underwater, be honest — but a financial-distress seller may have paths (short sale) worth naming.

---

### "I want to think about it" / "I'm not ready to decide"

**What's really being said:** Either a real need for time, or a soft no. Find out which without pressure.

**Response (Sandler — give them permission to say no):**
> "Totally fair — this is a big decision and I'd never want you to rush it. So I'm clear, **what specifically would you want to think through?** Is it the number, the timing, or whether selling's the right move at all? And honestly — if the answer ends up being no, that's completely okay. I'd just rather know than chase you."

**Principle:** Sandler "negative reverse" — removing pressure paradoxically lowers defenses. The calibrated question surfaces the *real* hesitation (price vs timing vs commitment), which you can then address directly.

---

### "I need to talk to my spouse / kids / family"

**Response:**
> "Of course — everyone who needs to be part of this should be. **Who else is involved in the decision, and what do you think their biggest question will be?** If it'd help, I can put everything in writing so you've got the full picture to discuss. When would be a good time to reconnect once you've talked?"

**Principle:** Identify the real decision-makers (don't present to a non-decider). Surface their likely objection in advance. Set a concrete follow-up time (Sandler — no open-ended "I'll get back to you").

---

### "I have another offer / a realtor says I can list higher"

**Response:**
> "That's great — you should absolutely take the best path for you. The thing to weigh: a listing is a *hope* at a higher number, minus commissions, minus the months of showings, minus whatever repairs a retail buyer's inspector flags. Mine's a *certain* number, cash, as-is, closing in [14] days, nothing falls through. **If certainty and speed weren't a factor, what would make the listing route worth the wait for you?**"

**Principle:** Don't disparage the competing option. Reframe the comparison as *certain-now* vs *maybe-more-later-minus-costs*. HV's edge is speed + certainty, not top dollar — lean into it. The calibrated question makes them weigh the tradeoff out loud.

---

### "Can you do better?" / "Is that your best?"

**Response:**
> "I want to be straight with you — that number's not a negotiating tactic, it's where the math lands to make this work for me on the rehab and resale. **If there's something about the property I've got wrong — maybe the work's less than I think — show me and I'll re-run it on the spot.** But I'm not going to throw out a higher number just to get you to sign; that wouldn't be fair to either of us."

**Principle:** Hold the number with integrity (builds trust). Open the door to revising *inputs* (rehab) rather than just caving on price. This is exactly why the pre-offer-then-adjust workflow matters — you can re-run live.

---

### "I'm not in a hurry / no rush to sell"

**What's really being said:** Low motivation. May be a poor fit, or may be testing.

**Response:**
> "No problem at all — there's no pressure from me. Can I ask: **what got you to take the meeting today?** Even if the timing's not now, it's worth understanding what you're weighing, so when you *are* ready, we can move fast. Most of my sellers tell me the value was knowing they had a certain option in their back pocket."

**Principle:** Don't manufacture urgency. Re-probe motivation (why take the meeting at all?). Position as a standing option. If genuinely no motivation, this is a candidate to politely close out — don't burn time.

---

### "Why is your offer so much less than what I paid / what it's worth?"

**Response:**
> "I hear you — it's hard when the number's below what the place means to you or what you put into it. Here's the honest version: I have to buy it at a price where, after I put [$X] of work in and pay the costs to resell, there's a margin left. **The gap you're feeling is mostly the rehab** — if the work were half what I estimated, my number would be a lot closer to yours. **What part of the condition do you think I'm overestimating?**"

**Principle:** Empathy first (Voss — acknowledge the emotional anchor). Attribute the gap to rehab (the defensible, non-personal factor), not to lowballing. Invite them to challenge the rehab estimate — which you can re-run.

## How to deploy in the brief

For each appointment, the skill picks objections by posture:

| Seller posture | Likely objections to prep |
|---|---|
| Asking >15% above MAO | "Too low", "Zillow says more", "Can you do better" |
| Financial distress + underwater | "I owe more than that" |
| Low motivation / "just testing" | "Not in a hurry", "Want to think about it" |
| Inherited / multiple heirs | "Need to talk to family" |
| Listed with agent | "I have another offer / can list higher" |
| Sentimental (raised kids, long-time owner) | "Why so much less than it's worth" |

Include the framed response, not just the objection name. Cap at 3 in the brief — the franchisee can ask for more on the spot.

## Anti-patterns

- Never script a manipulative or high-pressure close. These responses build trust and surface truth; they don't trap the seller.
- Never argue the ARV. Redirect to rehab and certainty every time.
- Never invent flexibility you don't have. If the deal can't move, say so.
- Never present to a non-decision-maker without surfacing who the real decider is.
